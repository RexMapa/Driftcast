The insight

Meditation apps ask you to clear your mind. Anxious minds don't clear — they need somewhere else to go.

There's a well-documented reason bored, low-stakes audio — a droning history lecture, a stranger reading Wikipedia, rain against a window — puts anxious people to sleep faster than a breathing exercise: it gives a racing mind a less threatening thing to chew on, a technique sleep researchers call cognitive shuffling. It doesn't fight the noise in your head. It changes the channel.

Podcast listeners already do this by instinct — falling asleep to true crime, history, or ASMR they half-hunted for at 11pm. Driftcast just closes the gap between "here's what's actually bothering me tonight" and "here's forty minutes of something calmer to listen to instead."

How it works

Three steps, no scrolling required at midnight

01

Tell it what's looping

Type or speak the actual thought — not a mood. "Rent's going up again" curates differently than "annoyed at my sister."

02

It curates, not generates

A short original bridge acknowledges the worry, then hands off to real episodes matched across Spotify, YouTube, SoundCloud and podcast RSS by tone and topic.

03

It mixes you under

Rain, brown noise or a fireplace fades in beneath the voice and takes over on a timer once you've likely gone.

The design

Five screens, one mood

Midnight surfaces, a single amethyst accent, and monospace numerals wherever something is playing, counting, or timed — the vocabulary of a podcast player, not a meditation app.

What's actually built

Five pieces, none of them a meditation timer

i.

The Worry Box

One free-text field instead of a mood grid. Understands "presentation tomorrow" and "rent going up" as different problems needing different audio, not the same "stressed" button.

ii.

Cross-platform curation

Matches against Spotify, YouTube, SoundCloud and open podcast RSS feeds by topic and vocal tone. Driftcast never hosts or re-uploads audio — it links out and plays via each platform's own SDK.

iii.

The bridge

A short, original spoken line that acknowledges the specific worry before handing off — the one piece of audio Driftcast actually generates, kept under 90 seconds on purpose.

iv.

The soundscape mixer

Rain, brown noise, piano, fireplace — independent layers under any source, with a sleep timer and gradual fade so the mix doesn't outlast the listener.

Go-to-market

The marketing plan

For the person still awake replaying today, Driftcast is the sleep app that starts by asking what's actually wrong — then hands you real, curated audio to fall asleep to instead of another guided breathing exercise. Unlike Calm or Headspace, it doesn't ask you to clear your mind. It gives it somewhere to go.
Who it's for

Three people, one habit

The Ruminator

28–42 · law, consulting, healthcare, startups

Phone in hand at 11pm, replaying the day instead of scrolling it away. Tried Calm or Headspace, let the subscription lapse — the meditations felt generic, sometimes patronizing.

The Podcast Sleeper

22–38 · already asleep to true crime or history

Has a genre that works but hunts for the right episode most nights. Wants the habit kept, minus the searching at 1am with 4% battery.

The New Parent

30–45 · irregular windows, low effort tolerance

Might have eleven minutes before the next wake-up. Values "type it and go" over any onboarding flow longer than a single screen.
